## 0.1.0 — Repository scaffold

Turned the single-file browser prototype into a governed TypeScript project. No gameplay is implemented yet.

### Governance

- Initialized the Git repository and pinned `game-devkit` v0.10.2 (`3252e00`) as the `dev/foundation/` submodule.
- Selected the `web-react` platform with no profiles. Recorded the no-React deviation in `dev/standards/project_structure.addendum.md`: the project is TypeScript plus Canvas 2D, `src/ui/` is plain DOM, and the React, IndexedDB, service-worker, and PWA triggers do not fire here.
- Scaffolded the three required operation contracts, then replaced the placeholder text in `dev/agent_rules/test_operations.md` with the real environment, layers, commands, and pass criteria.
- Declared `port-ref/` as a project-owned root tree holding the original prototype as read-only reference material.
- Recorded the V1 lifecycle reduction in `dev/docs/README.md`: the design document is the draft, and work goes straight to standalone implementation specs.
- 2026-07-27 — [code_style] Added `dev/standards/code_style.addendum.md`: a branch chain over a closed enumeration ends in a compiler-proved exception branch, never an unguarded fallthrough yielding one of the members. TypeScript is the only enforcement, because the linter runs without type information.

### Toolchain

- Vite, TypeScript, Vitest, Prettier, oxlint, and dependency-cruiser. 99 packages; no UI framework, no renderer library, no desktop shell.
- `npm run verify` implements the command surface standard's six stages in order: format check → typecheck → lint → boundary check → unit tests → build. Green.
- `npm run check:governance` runs the local contract checker and the foundation consumer verifier. Both report OK.
- Encoded the design document's layer boundaries as machine-checked rules, including the ones for layers not yet earned, so earning a layer later starts from an enforced boundary rather than an open door.

### Product

- Design document v1.3 at `dev/docs/design/pantry-depths_v1.md`. Five floors, `max(0, attack − defense)` on both sides, three key colours mapped to route / attack / defense, a forced-route budget of 90 of 120 HP, and a `Frozen extensions` list that scopes V1.
- v1.2 moved enemy art from procedural Canvas drawing to fixed 512×512 sprite files, with the authoring spec, per-enemy `scale` and `anchorY`, the distance-tint requirement, and the pre-baked hit flash. Environment surfaces stay procedural.
- v1.3 settled the fiction against the project name: the dungeon is a manor's five-level cellar and the player descends rather than climbs. Floor themes became wine cellar / ice cellar / meat larder / guard level / deepest storeroom, and the stair tile characters now follow the roguelike convention (`>` down, `<` up). Themes affect texture palette and decor only; no rule changes.
- Moved the original prototype (`game.js`, `index.html`, `style.css`) into `port-ref/`. Its mouse-look, gold, chests, potions, and inventory are cut by the design document and will not be ported.

### Feel and Interface

- 2026-07-27 — [pantry_feel_01] Shipped the playable keyboard run: discrete commands resolve through one seam, settle exactly once at presentation start, and drive interpolated camera motion, held-forward repeat, and refusal feedback. This is the first consumer of the renderer's semantic-event path, which closes the deferred evidence `pantry_presentation` left open.
- 2026-07-27 — [pantry_controls] Movement split from facing: W and S step along the eyes, A and D sidestep without turning, Q and E turn, F interacts, and left click spends the faced cell. A one-cell lateral offset costs one input instead of a turn, a step, and a turn back. Backward movement is no longer refused, so the retreat cost that "cannot walk backwards" produced is gone as a mechanic; the pointer leans the drawn view only and never changes the discrete facing every rule reads.
- 2026-07-27 — [pantry_run_readout] The run is now legible. Health, attack, defense, key counts, and floor are always on screen; facing an enemy names it and shows its health bar, raw numbers, and whether the sword can penetrate at all; each point of health lost appears as a number, and a retaliation defense absorbed entirely reports as blocked. A finished run reports its deepest floor, final stats, and doors opened behind a keyboard-reachable restart, replacing a death that simply stopped accepting input.
- 2026-07-27 — [pantry_run_readout] The minimap reveals the current floor outright rather than tracking exploration, so routes are plannable and corners hide nothing. It stays a projection of world data intersected with entity activity — opened doors, collected keys, and defeated enemies vanish with no bookkeeping — and secrets stay off it: an intact breakable wall reads as ordinary stone.
- 2026-07-27 — [presentation] Corrected the projection. The camera plane now derives from the canvas aspect instead of a fixed angle, ending a horizontal stretch of about 1.4x that cost the player the side walls at a junction and made a quarter turn sweep far more screen than it represented. Floor tiles now draw their cell boundary heavier than the flagstone joints inside them, so counting cells to judge distance is reliable.
- 2026-07-27 — [content] An enemy is identified by its archetype rather than by its artwork. Four archetypes share one placeholder appearance, so an entity carrying only its appearance could not say what it was; resolving artwork at the presentation boundary also retires a cast that would have thrown for every archetype borrowing shared art.

### Demo Enemies

- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] Pursuit and attacking became two systems that never run in the same frame. A body holds the one distance band its archetype declares, and any live attack state — winding up, striking, or recovering from the cooldown — holds it still and is told apart from the others by the body alone. The cooldown was already a standstill and is now a visible one, which is what turns the most punishable window in a fight into something the player can hunt for.
- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] A body with no wind-up now has no attack at all: the slimes' contact damage and the shooter's melee poke are gone, and with nothing able to suppress their pursuit the slimes advance forever. The shooter's strafe-on-cooldown went with them — standing exposed is the price of a long reload. A skeleton clubbed mid-swing also stops showing the raised sword.

- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] The slime is three entities rather than one body wearing three colours. Green, blue, and red carry their own health, drawn size, footprint, and shove, monotonic so the colour tells the player what they are looking at. The footprint is one number driving the blob, the push, and the target a thrown weapon has to hit; wall clearance stays one number for every body, because a body that cannot fit through a doorway cannot block one either.
- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] A soft body drops nothing at all, which leaves the walls and the things carrying weapons as the only two sources on a floor. Bombs keep every behaviour they have and simply cannot be found. What spawns is now two rolls — what kind of thing this is, then which of them — so a new type joins one list without quietly taking floor space from the other.

- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] A clip carries its own frame count, and its sheet is derived from that count rather than stored beside it, so a row cannot contradict itself and the loader checks each atlas against its own shape instead of one size for the batch. The offline generator bakes to the same table, the workbench scrubs every clip at its own width, and the frame count stops being a fixed price every clip pays whether it moves or not.

- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] The thrown axe is a hammer, and it is the demolition tool bombs are being taken away from. Bodies neither stop it nor survive it, whatever their health; what it counts is masonry, opening up to three walls and flying on through each opening. A barricade, an emplacement, the outer boundary, or the floor under a downward throw takes the whole budget at once, and the hammer is gone wherever it stopped. A throw touching the floor is a stopping condition the demo did not previously have at all.

- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] The death set is shared content owned by no skeleton type, classified by the situation that killed the body rather than by the injury it depicts. Collapsing and drowning run their full length, an opened body comes apart over four frames, and a body driven into masonry or run through is one held pose with a burst of bones doing the rest. A body a bomb reached has no corpse at all by design, and the bones scatter from the single kill exit so no route out of the world can forget them. A skeleton no longer leaves a mark on the wall it was thrown into, and the bones it scatters cannot be picked up.
- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] The entity workbench gained a third coverage state for artwork that is absent on purpose, so a permanent false alarm stopped being the only way to report it, and lost the borrowed state entirely now that every situation owns its own clip. The two situation probes ask whether a death is depicted at all rather than asserting the shape a soft body takes.

- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] Four authored bodies ship, each privately owning only the clips its own weapon is in: standing, walking, being hurt, being stunned, winding up, striking, and recovering. The one attack clip became three, so a body about to swing, a body swinging, and a body gathering itself are told apart by the body alone. A wind-up reaches its final pose quickly and holds it, so the same four frames read at one second and at three, and a recovery runs the same curve back. Whole set decoded: 281 MB across the four types, against 168 MB for the single body it replaces.
- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] The offline generator takes a type and a weapon and loops all four, baking the death set and the loose pickups once. Hammer, javelin, and crossbow joined the sword as authored parts of the body rather than as a layer over it. The entity workbench previews the three attack states separately, each at the length the simulation actually gives it, and the stun stopped being one frame held until it wore off.

- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] A floor is three slimes and four skeletons. The hammer-bearer inherits the charger's whole arrangement and stops being a slime; the javelineer and the crossbowman are one behaviour with two rhythms — a long commitment that repeats often against a short one that rarely comes again. Neither has a contact attack, so walking up to one and standing in its face is safe. The two attacking slimes are gone: a slime does not attack, and the bodies that had those behaviours are skeletons now.
- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] The numbers describing a shot moved onto the type that fires it, so a javelin can be slower, heavier, and shove the player off the ground they were holding while a bolt stays fast and cheap. Every skeleton rolls one drop shape with its own weapon in the last column, and a dropped crossbow fires three times before the stock is all that is left. The entity workbench draws a body's footprint and its wall clearance at once, because they are deliberately different numbers.

- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] Played, and retuned against what playing showed. Every body carries roughly double the health, so no fight is settled by one landed throw. The two shooters lost their cooldowns entirely and are paced by the ground they hold instead — four cells for the javelineer against eight for the crossbowman, each backing away below half its own band — and both turn to face what they are aiming at, which they never did while standing still. A cooldown no longer freezes the body that is serving it: a skeleton between attacks chases as usual and simply cannot swing, which is what the cooldown costs it.
- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] A slime stops short of the player instead of walking into them, and what it costs to push past one is pace rather than position. The shove it used to give was a force arguing with the player's own input sixty times a second, which read as a body twitching rather than as a crowd being in the way. Three reversals of the plan this shipped under, taken deliberately after playing it: a slime is now a block you can wade through rather than a push, it gained a behaviour the plan said it would not, and it no longer moves the player at all.
- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] A stun now takes away whatever the body was committing to. It used to set a timer and nothing else, so a skeleton clubbed mid-swing kept its telegraph painted on the floor, waited the stun out, and finished the attack at the spot the player had been standing when they threw the thing — the interruption cost it time and nothing else. Every warning on the floor and over the head disappears on its own now, because all of them read the wind-up that is no longer there.
- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] The dev panel grew three commands and a key each: empty the floor through the ordinary death path, refill the crowd to its spawn cap, and drop one of every pickup within reach — including the worn-out forms, which are otherwise reached only by finding a weapon and using it up. Every command names its key, because the panel's buttons cannot be clicked while the pointer is locked and that is most of play.
- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] A prop says whether its count is separate objects or shots inside one, so a crossbow holding three stopped being drawn as three crossbows. A thrown crossbow stock also has a picture for the first time: it declared itself a rod, which the sprite pass skips, and the beam pass knew the hammer and the sword by name and nothing else — so it had been invisible for its whole flight.
- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] Deferred evidence, accepted at closeout: the baked poses have never been judged. Both re-bake passes owned structure and a bake that runs end to end, never finished animation, and one look has already found that every clip but standing and walking reads wrong. Whether the three attack states are told apart by the body alone, whether a recovery reads as free hits at six seconds as well as at 1.8, and whether every clip scrubs at its own frame count are all unanswered; `TODO.md` carries the pass under Draft. Two shooters told apart by rhythm alone is no longer true either, and deliberately so — both now take three seconds and are separated by the ground they hold.

### The Demo Floor Loop

- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_01] Blessings became a two-tier ladder and stopped having a third case. The authored tier is drawn first and still never repeats; once it is spent the draw falls to a stacking tier of maximum health, melee damage, movement speed, and melee reach, each awarded under its own name and card and each compounding with every repeat. The single fixed consolation health bonus is gone as a concept, so a late run no longer receives the same reward under four labels. Movement speed and melee reach accumulate unread — the shared modifier catalogue is where they get wired.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_02] A demo floor became five blocks instead of one: a twenty-one-square main region carrying the arrival and the descent, with a seven-square room attached to each of its four sides. The floor stays square, and deliberately so — every reader of its dimension outside floor generation is a loop bound or a flat index that survives one number changing and not two. Block rings are boundary brick, so the assembly keeps its shape however hard the player swings and the only ways between blocks are the punched doorways; each room is open floor throughout, holds one piece of business, and is guaranteed walkable from the moment the player arrives. Which side each room lands on is drawn per floor, because the room a run is left from is never marked and a fixed side would be learned once. Hazards stay in the main region.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_03] The side rooms took up their business. The plinth the floor has always had is now the cursed altar and stands in a room of its own; a second altar is claimed by holding its room for five unbroken seconds, which damage does not interrupt and only leaving cancels, so the claim is won by clearing the room rather than by not being hit; and a third room gives health back to whoever stands in it. What each room asks of the player is the only thing that tells them apart — no room carries a label. The two new rooms announce themselves and throw off embers and steam rather than standing anything up, because what a floor builds is drawn by a module a concurrent rewrite owns.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_04] A floor now owes something before it will let the player leave. One main task opens the descent and reveals where it is on the map, and pays nothing by itself; three secondary tasks each pay a blessing the moment they are met. Taking the stairs no longer pays a blessing at all, which was the reward that made the cheapest run the one that touched as little of each floor as possible. Every condition is a total the simulation already kept — bodies, broken masonry, side rooms entered, pools closed over — so no system had to be reached into to observe one, and a floor states what it wants on the message line because that is the one channel to the screen a concurrent rewrite does not own. The descent is still drawn in the first-person view; only the map withholds it.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_05] The run carries one difficulty level, on screen at all times, rising by one each minute and by five each descent. It is derived from the clock and the depth rather than stored, so the number and its sources cannot drift, and no descent resets it — a clock that reset would make descending a way to buy difficulty back. The two rates together price a floor at five minutes, which is the exchange rate a player is deciding against every time they take a secondary task. Nothing but the display reads the level; the tables that would carry it into enemy statistics are being rewritten elsewhere, and wiring it in is the first thing to do after that lands.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_06] One catalogue now owns every numeric axis a run can move, and the stacking blessings read it instead of carrying their own copy. Each axis states what one blessing on it is worth, what a clean core rolls, and what a cursed core rolls over a range widened at both ends — better than clean at the top and worse than nothing at the bottom, so the range itself is the curse and no separate table of drawbacks is needed. Melee damage, maximum health, movement speed, and melee reach are all wired: each is read through one accessor, so a new source of modifiers reaches every axis at once. Thrown damage stays defined as melee damage and inherits the lot. Four melee bases are authored as a table with no runtime behaviour yet, named for what they are in a cellar rather than for a body's weapon.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_07] A run can now be left rather than only lost. Finishing a floor's main task and smashing the cursed altar each hand over a sealed reward that says only where it came from; what it is, and what it rolled, is decided at the extraction room and nowhere earlier, so a run halfway through genuinely does not know what it is carrying. Walking into the extraction room ends the run, opens everything carried, and banks it; dying loses the lot, which is what makes the room worth finding on the first floor rather than the last. A core carried out is what the next run swings with — its base and its rolls change melee damage, reach, and starting health — and the run reads which core and which way its rolls went, because a curse that can roll worse than clean is only a curse if that is visible. The cursed altar pays a curse now instead of a blessing.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop] Every side room now has something standing in it, and nothing on the map says which room is which. The cursed altar's stone turned dark red and the light off it followed, so the one fixture that pays a curse stops looking like part of the building; the blessing altar is a pale dais with four corner posts, shaped to be stood in rather than walked around, and its light widens as the five-second hold accumulates; the hot spring is a tiered fountain that steams; and the extraction room holds one venting canister and a column of green smoke. Nothing a side room holds is drawn through a wall or marked on the minimap any more — the floating rune over the altar is gone entirely, and the stairs' through-wall shaft now appears only once the main task has opened the descent. An emitter can carry its own colour, which is what let green smoke exist without a second kind of plume.
