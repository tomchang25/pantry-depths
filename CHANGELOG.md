## 0.1.0 — Repository scaffold

Turned the single-file browser prototype into a governed TypeScript project. No gameplay is implemented yet.

### Governance

- Initialized the Git repository and pinned `game-devkit` v0.10.2 (`3252e00`) as the `dev/foundation/` submodule.
- Selected the `web-react` platform with no profiles. Recorded the no-React deviation in `dev/standards/project_structure.addendum.md`: the project is TypeScript plus Canvas 2D, `src/ui/` is plain DOM, and the React, IndexedDB, service-worker, and PWA triggers do not fire here.
- Scaffolded the three required operation contracts, then replaced the placeholder text in `dev/agent_rules/test_operations.md` with the real environment, layers, commands, and pass criteria.
- Declared `port-ref/` as a project-owned root tree holding the original prototype as read-only reference material.
- Recorded the V1 lifecycle reduction in `dev/docs/README.md`: the design document is the draft, and work goes straight to standalone implementation specs.
- 2026-07-27 — [code_style] Added `dev/standards/code_style.addendum.md`: a branch chain over a closed enumeration ends in a compiler-proved exception branch, never an unguarded fallthrough yielding one of the members. TypeScript is the only enforcement, because the linter runs without type information.

### Toolchain

- Vite, TypeScript, Vitest, Prettier, oxlint, and dependency-cruiser. 99 packages; no UI framework, no renderer library, no desktop shell.
- `npm run verify` implements the command surface standard's six stages in order: format check → typecheck → lint → boundary check → unit tests → build. Green.
- `npm run check:governance` runs the local contract checker and the foundation consumer verifier. Both report OK.
- Encoded the design document's layer boundaries as machine-checked rules, including the ones for layers not yet earned, so earning a layer later starts from an enforced boundary rather than an open door.

### Product

- Design document v1.3 at `dev/docs/design/pantry-depths_v1.md`. Five floors, `max(0, attack − defense)` on both sides, three key colours mapped to route / attack / defense, a forced-route budget of 90 of 120 HP, and a `Frozen extensions` list that scopes V1.
- v1.2 moved enemy art from procedural Canvas drawing to fixed 512×512 sprite files, with the authoring spec, per-enemy `scale` and `anchorY`, the distance-tint requirement, and the pre-baked hit flash. Environment surfaces stay procedural.
- v1.3 settled the fiction against the project name: the dungeon is a manor's five-level cellar and the player descends rather than climbs. Floor themes became wine cellar / ice cellar / meat larder / guard level / deepest storeroom, and the stair tile characters now follow the roguelike convention (`>` down, `<` up). Themes affect texture palette and decor only; no rule changes.
- Moved the original prototype (`game.js`, `index.html`, `style.css`) into `port-ref/`. Its mouse-look, gold, chests, potions, and inventory are cut by the design document and will not be ported.

- 2026-07-30 — [skeletons] The thrown axe is a hammer, and it is the demolition tool bombs are being taken away from. ### The Demo Floor Loop

- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_01] Blessings became a two-tier ladder and stopped having a third case. The authored tier is drawn first and still never repeats; once it is spent the draw falls to a stacking tier of maximum health, melee damage, movement speed, and melee reach, each awarded under its own name and card and each compounding with every repeat. The single fixed consolation health bonus is gone as a concept, so a late run no longer receives the same reward under four labels. Movement speed and melee reach accumulate unread — the shared modifier catalogue is where they get wired.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_02] A demo floor became five blocks instead of one: a twenty-one-square main region carrying the arrival and the descent, with a seven-square room attached to each of its four sides. The floor stays square, and deliberately so — every reader of its dimension outside floor generation is a loop bound or a flat index that survives one number changing and not two. Block rings are boundary brick, so the assembly keeps its shape however hard the player swings and the only ways between blocks are the punched doorways; each room is open floor throughout, holds one piece of business, and is guaranteed walkable from the moment the player arrives. Which side each room lands on is drawn per floor, because the room a run is left from is never marked and a fixed side would be learned once. Hazards stay in the main region.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_03] The side rooms took up their business. The plinth the floor has always had is now the cursed altar and stands in a room of its own; a second altar is claimed by holding its room for five unbroken seconds, which damage does not interrupt and only leaving cancels, so the claim is won by clearing the room rather than by not being hit; and a third room gives health back to whoever stands in it. What each room asks of the player is the only thing that tells them apart — no room carries a label. The two new rooms announce themselves and throw off embers and steam rather than standing anything up, because what a floor builds is drawn by a module a concurrent rewrite owns.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_04] A floor now owes something before it will let the player leave. One main task opens the descent and reveals where it is on the map, and pays nothing by itself; three secondary tasks each pay a blessing the moment they are met. Taking the stairs no longer pays a blessing at all, which was the reward that made the cheapest run the one that touched as little of each floor as possible. Every condition is a total the simulation already kept — bodies, broken masonry, side rooms entered, pools closed over — so no system had to be reached into to observe one, and a floor states what it wants on the message line because that is the one channel to the screen a concurrent rewrite does not own. The descent is still drawn in the first-person view; only the map withholds it.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_05] The run carries one difficulty level, on screen at all times, rising by one each minute and by five each descent. It is derived from the clock and the depth rather than stored, so the number and its sources cannot drift, and no descent resets it — a clock that reset would make descending a way to buy difficulty back. The two rates together price a floor at five minutes, which is the exchange rate a player is deciding against every time they take a secondary task. Nothing but the display reads the level; the tables that would carry it into enemy statistics are being rewritten elsewhere, and wiring it in is the first thing to do after that lands.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_06] One catalogue now owns every numeric axis a run can move, and the stacking blessings read it instead of carrying their own copy. Each axis states what one blessing on it is worth, what a clean core rolls, and what a cursed core rolls over a range widened at both ends — better than clean at the top and worse than nothing at the bottom, so the range itself is the curse and no separate table of drawbacks is needed. Melee damage, maximum health, movement speed, and melee reach are all wired: each is read through one accessor, so a new source of modifiers reaches every axis at once. Thrown damage stays defined as melee damage and inherits the lot. Four melee bases are authored as a table with no runtime behaviour yet, named for what they are in a cellar rather than for a body's weapon.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop_07] A run can now be left rather than only lost. Finishing a floor's main task and smashing the cursed altar each hand over a sealed reward that says only where it came from; what it is, and what it rolled, is decided at the extraction room and nowhere earlier, so a run halfway through genuinely does not know what it is carrying. Walking into the extraction room ends the run, opens everything carried, and banks it; dying loses the lot, which is what makes the room worth finding on the first floor rather than the last. A core carried out is what the next run swings with — its base and its rolls change melee damage, reach, and starting health — and the run reads which core and which way its rolls went, because a curse that can roll worse than clean is only a curse if that is visible. The cursed altar pays a curse now instead of a blessing.
- 2026-07-30 — [pantry_demo_floor_loop] Every side room now has something standing in it, and nothing on the map says which room is which. The cursed altar's stone turned dark red and the light off it followed, so the one fixture that pays a curse stops looking like part of the building; the blessing altar is a pale dais with four corner posts, shaped to be stood in rather than walked around, and its light widens as the five-second hold accumulates; the hot spring is a tiered fountain that steams; and the extraction room holds one venting canister and a column of green smoke. Nothing a side room holds is drawn through a wall or marked on the minimap any more — the floating rune over the altar is gone entirely, and the stairs' through-wall shaft now appears only once the main task has opened the descent. An emitter can carry its own colour, which is what let green smoke exist without a second kind of plume.

### Telegraphed Threats And Directional Damage

- 2026-07-30 — [telegraphs_01] A committed attack resolves against the point it was aimed at when its wind-up began, instead of re-picking the player's position at the instant it fires. The shot's direction, the charge's direction, and the lane drawn under a charger all read that one recorded point, so a warning stopped describing the present and started describing the future — and stepping two paces sideways became a counter where cover had been the only one. Contact melee is untouched, because it already re-checks range at resolution and misses a player who left.
- 2026-07-30 — [telegraphs_02] Each wind-up wears its own symbol — a reticle for a shot, a flame for a charge, a blade for a cut — all in one warning red, so the colour says that something is about to hit you and the shape says what to do about it. The mark used to be chosen by asking whether the attack was a charge, which had a swordsman raising its sword wearing the shooter's badge. A shooter also swells and lights as the shot gathers, and runs a line of beads to its locked point that stops at the first thing that would stop the bolt, so "step out of the line" and "put a wall in it" are both readable without a HUD.
- 2026-07-30 — [telegraphs_03] The charger became a thing the player positions rather than a thing that hits them. Its wind-up went from 0.8 to 3.0 seconds, which removes it as a threat outright, and it pays for that by becoming worth wanting: a charge breaks the masonry it slams into, and a charge that stalls leaves the charger stunned for five seconds. While it gathers it crouches, pours embers, and carries a reddening light the whole room can see. The floor chevrons became one red strip laid down the locked lane, dim along its full length from the moment the lane commits and swept bright as the wind-up counts down — one object carrying both where and when.
- 2026-07-30 — [telegraphs_04] Stun has a picture: three stars orbit above any enemy whose stun timer is running, whatever put it there, on every archetype rather than only the slimes — the state is one state, and a cue that appears on some bodies and not others reads as a bug. They are world-space billboards at real positions, so whatever hides the body hides them. The swordsman, an authored sheet with nothing to squash, shows its wind-up instead as a brightening arc swept along the ground the cut will cross, which is the more useful half of a body cue anyway.
- 2026-07-30 — [telegraphs_05] A hit from a known source raises a soft arc on the crosshair pointing at where it came from, recomputed every frame from the player's current facing, so turning toward the source sweeps the arc away and several live marks stack into a picture of being surrounded. What is stored is a world position and never a screen angle: a mark that holds its place on screen while the player turns points at somewhere the threat is not, which is worse than the full-screen red flash it replaces.
- 2026-07-30 — [telegraphs_06] Every floor carries two or three breakable mortar emplacements that fight everyone alike. One picks a body at random from everything beyond its two-tile dead zone — player or enemy, with no preference — locks the ground that body was standing on, paints a circle there with a column of beads at its centre, and shells the mark five seconds later over any wall in the way. Everything inside the blast takes it, and a kill the shell makes counts as the player's, so positioning yourself where the floor's own artillery does the work is a way of playing it. The dead zone is the counter: walking up to one and breaking it down is always safe.
- 2026-07-30 — [telegraphs] Floor warnings are painted into the floor rather than laid on top of it. A decal channel shapes discs, rings, and oriented lanes at sub-cell resolution and shades and fogs each one as part of the ground it belongs to, so the charger's lane and the shell's landing mark stopped lifting off the stone and a circle holds its shape from any angle.
- 2026-07-30 — [telegraphs] A sword cut resolves as the wedge it draws rather than as a circle around the swordsman, and the body holds still and pointed where it committed for the whole wind-up — so stepping around a raised sword works. The wedge is painted into the floor with the blade arc and the overhead mark sweeping across it, and every warning is now worn at the height of the head it belongs to.
- 2026-07-30 — [telegraphs] How large a wind-up mark is drawn and how far it swells became authored content in its own right, tuned apart from the body wearing it, and each archetype declares what its wind-up commits to — so the symbol set is derived from the archetypes rather than assembled at the point of drawing, and an archetype that never winds up carries no mark to get wrong.

### The Demo Tool Chain

- 2026-07-30 — [editor] Every workbench draws through one embeddable render panel that owns its canvas, its frame loop, its resize, its error state, and its teardown, and shares presentation image loading across concurrent instances. That is the whole point of the tool chain: the lab it replaces drew a hand-made corridor, and a display number is wrong only in the medium that can show it.
- 2026-07-30 — [editor] The Entity Workbench reaches a body through three independent axes — archetype, situation, and state — instead of a list of preset scenarios, and runs every clip and every corpse on one scrubber at the duration the game itself gives it. It shares the living, death, carried-body, and barricade projection paths with the demo rather than imitating them, derives its wall, water, and barricade scenes from the demo's own hazard geometry, and reports coverage as a measured archetype-by-state matrix: artwork that was never authored shows as a gap instead of silently falling back to standing still. That matrix is the half with no other home — nothing else in the project will tell you what is missing.
- 2026-07-30 — [editor] The HUD moved behind a pure display model that the demo derives and a workbench tab feeds by hand, so how a bar reads at three health can be judged without being hit down to three health. Melee poses became authored content that the game and the tool read from one file, and every authored value in the project now saves through a single whitelisted development endpoint with one validator per target rather than each tool growing an endpoint of its own. `src/ui/` was deleted in the same pass: the HUD stays in `src/demo/`, where the rule that this half is verified by playing it applies to it.
- 2026-07-30 — [editor] Decor is authored on its own tab — named wall and tile variants previewed in the shared renderer, validated and saved through the common endpoint, with a preset's internal offsets kept separate from where the piece is placed on a map, so what an author assembles in the tool is what the game assembles.
- 2026-07-30 — [editor] The tabs that followed took the rest of what a body and a loose object look like out of source. Body scale and wind-up marker offset became a validated content table tuned against a camera-distance slider with the cut's wedge drawn underneath, so size is never judged apart from reach; each loose prop carries its own lying size and float height, previewed one against a stack of three; and a carried prop's size and turn in the hand are tuned at rest and mid-throw, since a turn that reads still can spin wrong on release. Saving no longer reloads the page or resets a tool mid-tune, and every workbench can read its canonical file back on demand.

### The Map And Room Editor

- 2026-08-01 — [map_editor] A map is edited as the floor it assembles. The Map Workbench holds a map's slots, pool and draw count beside two views of one draw: the floor from above, with each room labelled by the file it came from and the arrival and the way out marked, and the same floor from inside at the game's own eye height, aimed down its longest clear line rather than at whichever wall a run would have faced. Both come from one call to the world the game builds, so re-drawing moves them together, and every refusal reports from inside that chain rather than from a second opinion beside it — a map that contradicts itself cannot be saved, and a draw that leaves no route to the way out says so and can be drawn again.
- 2026-08-01 — [map_editor] The map and room directories stopped being watched, and the two jobs the watcher was doing were given owners. A save now drops what the development server holds about the files it changed without telling any open page, so saving no longer reloads the tool that saved; and the authoring endpoint answers what a library directory holds right now, so a room created while the tool is open appears in it when asked rather than when the server restarts. An assembled room also carries the identity of the room file it was built from, which is the only way anything can say which of a pool's rooms landed in which slot.
- 2026-08-01 — [map_editor] A room is authored on its own, on a second tab beside the map. Everything a room file may declare short of its own cells is a control there — its extent and role, the crowd it keeps and how fast that crowd is replaced, the water, caltrops, emplacements and ground kit scattered into it, and how open the carve leaves it and what its walls are made of — and every quantity can be written as an exact number or as a range without the other spelling being rewritten under it, which matters because a range consumes a random number where a bare number does not. Absence stays a statement rather than a zero: a room that ticks nothing is built empty. A room is previewed as a floor of one room by the assembler that will build it, and a room whose cells are authored opens, previews and saves unchanged while saying that painting them is still to come.
- 2026-08-01 — [map_editor] A room whose cells are authored is painted rather than typed. Nine tile kinds on a palette, a grid the size of the room, click to place and drag to draw a run, and what is painted stands in the assembled room without a save in between. A room can be switched to authored cells and back, and changing its extent carries the grid with it — re-drawing the wall at the new boundary rather than leaving the old one standing inside the room, which is what turned a widened room into a sealed one. The refusal that matters here now reports while painting instead of at save: ground sealed off behind water or a trench is ground the runtime repair cannot reach, and an author needs to see it while the change that caused it is still in front of them.
- 2026-08-01 — [map_editor] A map or a room can be started from nothing and copied. Starting from nothing empties the draft to a template that is deliberately not yet legal — a map with no main region, a room with no identity — so the first thing on screen is what still has to be decided. Copying keeps the draft and moves it onto a free name, writes nothing until it is saved, and leaves the file it came from alone. And a save now says which of the two things it is about to do: creating a file and destroying one were indistinguishable until the moment they happened, which mattered little while the only way to make a file was to rename an old one and mattered a great deal once starting from nothing was offered. This is what acceptance criteria 1 and 2 had asked for since the plan was written; the first two children were closed against them on the strength of renaming, which writes a new file as a side effect of editing an old one and is not the same act.
- 2026-08-01 — [map_editor] Ground an author seals off is a design; ground a generator seals off is still a defect. A hand-painted room may now hold an island ringed in water — the room reader no longer refuses it, the assembly leaves an author's cells exactly as painted instead of cutting a channel through the moat, and the refusal on a built floor exempts them. Every generated room keeps the full guarantee, verified across twelve hundred assembled floors with no stranded ground. The editor says what was made — a sealed region costs three bodies a cell to reach — as an amber warning that never takes the save away, and painting itself became a stroke: the grid now survives its own edits, so holding the pointer down paints a run, with filled and trench recoloured apart from water on a palette the diagram shares.
- 2026-08-01 — [map_editor] The floor-set tool chain is gone: four debug tools, the floor schema and its validation, the provisional floor-set content, the run-state module only that schema still read, the offline generator, two commands, and every test whose subject they were — about six thousand lines that answered to a floor the game no longer plays. The authoring endpoint lost its floor-set target and with it its only generate operation; the key-colour type moved into the presentation file that actually uses it; the one browser spec now proves the hub by opening the Map Workbench. With this the map and room editor plan closed: every floor is authored in the tool that shows it being assembled, and nothing in the tree describes the old way.

### Flat Decoration Retired

- 2026-08-01 — [flat_decor] The decor preset chain is gone: the wall torch, the wall spikes, the floor bones and the hot-spring picture, the schema that validated them, the workbench tab that authored them, the authoring endpoint's decor target, and the four PNGs behind them. Not one of them was ever drawn by the game — the hot spring is built geometry and the bones are procedural fragments — so the whole vocabulary was a flat sticker library kept alive by the tool that edited it. What replaces it is what the altar and the stair mouth already are: things standing in the room with faces of their own.
- 2026-08-01 — [flat_decor] The lettered discs marking the way in and the way out are gone with them. The exit's disc had already been replaced by a sealed slab, its steps and its posts and had stopped being drawn at all; the entrance's was still on the ground, and a glyph painted flat where the player spawns is the last thing in the demo that read as a map legend rather than as a place. The stairs down are found by the shaft over them, and where a floor began is remembered by having walked out of it.
- 2026-08-01 — [flat_decor] The plan that would have declared wall marks and built structure as scene-contract channels is withdrawn unimplemented. It described the Canvas 2D renderer accurately, and both rules it wanted to write down — that a wall mark borrows a billboard's back-face culling, and that a cached floor is rebuilt when its terrain version moves — are artefacts of that renderer rather than of the game. A Three.js runtime has neither, so the contract entries would have been written and thrown away in the same quarter.
